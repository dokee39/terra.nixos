Grill: $ARGUMENTS

1. Check relevant files, system state, or docs first, only as deep as needed—never ask me for facts tools can verify. If a codebase or system is involved, look at existing entry points, similar patterns, and real constraints before asking.
2. Check the underlying problem before discussing implementation: consider what problem I actually need solved, whether my proposal jumps to a solution too early, and whether reusing what already exists, fixing an upstream cause, or doing nothing would be simpler. Ask about the goal only when it is unclear or questionable.
3. Work from broad choices down to details, settling prerequisites before choices that depend on them. Test hidden assumptions, failure cases, side effects, and how hard each choice is to undo.
4. Ask the single most important question for the current step and wait for my reply: give the options (if any) with your recommendation marked and a one-line reason in each; for open questions, give your recommended answer in 1–2 lines. No separate analysis or reason sections.
5. Stop asking once the remaining details will not change the direction or scope. Summarize the goal, chosen plan and scope, key constraints, rejected options, and open risks. Ask me to confirm, then stop.
6. Do not implement anything.
